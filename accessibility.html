Our commitment

A diner is meant to be the easiest place in a neighbourhood to walk into. That has to include people using a wheelchair, people who cannot see the menu board, people who read slowly, and people for whom a loud room is genuinely painful. We treat accessibility as part of running a restaurant properly rather than as a compliance exercise.

We are also not going to claim this site or our buildings are perfect. The known issues section below lists the things we know are wrong and what we are doing about each one.

Conformance standard

This website is built to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA. We believe the site is substantially conformant, with the exceptions noted below. Our restaurants are operated in accordance with the Americans with Disabilities Act.

What we have done on the website

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses real landmarks: a single main region, a header, a footer, and labelled navigation. Headings run in order without skipping levels.
  • Skip link. The first thing a keyboard user reaches on every page is a visible "Skip to main content" link.
  • Keyboard operable. Every control on the site can be reached and used with a keyboard alone, including the mobile menu, the More dropdown, the FAQ accordions, the gallery lightbox and the cookie banner. Nothing traps focus.
  • Visible focus. Focus is indicated with a three pixel teal outline and an offset, on every interactive element. We have not removed a single focus ring anywhere.
  • Colour contrast. Body text is at least 4.5:1 against its background and large text is at least 3:1. The cream on dark combination used in our night sections runs at roughly 16:1.
  • Colour is never the only signal. Dietary tags carry letters as well as colours. Form errors carry text as well as a red border.
  • Images. Every meaningful image has descriptive alternative text. Decorative background photography is marked as decorative so screen readers skip it.
  • Forms. Every field has a visible label, not a placeholder standing in for one. Errors appear inline next to the field, in text, and are also collected into a summary at the top of the form that moves focus and links to each problem field.
  • Status messages. Confirmation and error messages are announced to screen readers without stealing focus mid-task.
  • Motion. Everything that animates, including the neon flicker, is disabled when your system asks for reduced motion. The neon stays lit, it simply stops flickering.
  • Touch targets. Every button and link target is at least 44 by 44 pixels on touch screens.
  • Zoom and reflow. The site is usable at 400% zoom and at 320 pixels wide without horizontal scrolling. We do not disable pinch zoom.
  • No autoplay. There is no audio and no video that starts on its own anywhere on this site.
  • No blocking interstitials. The cookie banner sits at the bottom of the page and does not cover the content or prevent you reading it.

How we test

Before any change goes live we run through the site with a keyboard only, check every new page with the NVDA screen reader on Windows and VoiceOver on macOS, run an automated audit, and verify contrast ratios on any colour we have changed. We test at 320, 768, 1024 and 1440 pixels wide, and at 200% and 400% zoom.

Automated tools catch perhaps a third of real problems, so the manual keyboard and screen reader pass is the one that matters. A full independent audit is scheduled for the first quarter of 2027.

In the dining rooms

All three locations

  • Step free entry from the street or the parking lot.
  • An accessible restroom on the main floor, with a changing table.
  • At least four tables that accommodate a wheelchair without moving furniture. Ask for one when you book and we will hold it.
  • Aisles between booths of at least 36 inches.
  • Large print menus at every register, on request, no explanation needed.
  • Staff trained to read the menu aloud, describe dishes, and take an order at a slower pace.
  • Service animals welcome throughout.
  • A quieter corner in each room, away from the fountain and the pass, which we will seat you in on request.

Albuquerque, 2001 Central Avenue NW

  • Two accessible parking spaces adjacent to the rear entrance, both van accessible.
  • Fully step free apart from the patio door lip noted above.
  • The Chrome Room private space is step free and has its own accessible restroom.

Chicago, 1454 North Milwaukee Avenue

  • Step free entry from Milwaukee Avenue.
  • A permanent ramp to the raised booth section at the rear.
  • No dedicated parking. The nearest accessible metered spaces are on Honore Street, half a block north.

Santa Monica, 1447 2nd Street

  • Step free throughout, including the patio, which is level with the sidewalk.
  • Accessible parking in Structure 4 on 2nd Street, with step free access to street level.
  • The widest aisles of the three rooms, at 42 inches.

Menus in other formats

We will provide the menu in large print at any location on request. Braille menus are currently available in Albuquerque and Santa Monica, with Chicago to follow. If you would like the full menu emailed as plain text before your visit so you can read it with your own software, write to hello@starlitechrome.com and we will send it the same day.

The menu page on this site is plain HTML with real headings and lists, and is designed to be read comfortably with a screen reader or at high zoom.

Service animals

Service animals are welcome in every part of every restaurant, including the Chrome Room and the chef's table. Our staff are trained not to ask for documentation and not to ask what your disability is. There is water available for any working animal, just ask.

Tell us about a problem

If something on this site or in one of our rooms does not work for you, we want to know, and we will not treat it as a complaint to be managed. Accessibility reports go straight to the owner.
