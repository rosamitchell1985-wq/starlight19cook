What cookies and similar technologies are

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store. Local storage does a similar job with a larger capacity and no automatic expiry. Both let a site remember something between page loads, such as the fact that you already answered a question.

Cookies set by the site you are visiting are called first party. Cookies set by another company whose code runs on the page are called third party, and those are the ones that can follow you between websites.

How consent works on this site

No analytics or advertising cookie is set before you choose. On your first visit a banner appears at the bottom of the page with three options:

  • Accept all. Enables analytics and advertising cookies.
  • Reject non-essential. Nothing beyond the strictly necessary items below is set. The site works normally.
  • Manage preferences. Turn analytics and advertising on or off independently.

Your answer is stored in local storage under the key starlite-consent-v1 for 12 months, after which we ask again. That storage item is itself strictly necessary: without it we would have to show you the banner on every single page.

Rejecting everything does not reduce any feature of this site. Menus, reservations, online ordering, gift cards, the gallery and the journal all work identically. The only difference is that advertising will be less relevant and we will have less information about which pages are useful.

Browser controls

Every major browser lets you block or delete cookies independently of anything we do. The relevant settings are usually under Privacy or Site Settings. Blocking all cookies will break the consent banner itself, which means it will reappear on every page load, and it may affect other websites more seriously than it affects this one.

Most browsers also offer a private or incognito mode, which discards all cookies when the window is closed.

Global Privacy Control

If your browser or an extension sends a Global Privacy Control signal, we treat it as a valid instruction to reject analytics and advertising cookies, and as an opt out of sale and sharing under California law. You do not need to do anything else. See the Do Not Sell or Share section of our Privacy Policy.

Changes to this policy

If we add or remove a cookie, this page is updated and the date at the top changes. If we add a new category of tracking, we reset consent and ask you again rather than assuming your previous answer covers it.
