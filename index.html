Seventy years on Central Avenue

A diner that never turned the sign off

Merle and Dot Vasquez opened the first Starlite in 1954 in a converted Quonset hut on the Albuquerque stretch of Route 66. The griddle was seasoned with beef fat, the malts were spun in a green Hamilton Beach, and the neon out front buzzed all night for truckers heading west.

Seventy years later the recipe card has not changed much. We still grind chuck and short rib fresh every morning, still pull our own potatoes for fries, and still keep the counter open past midnight on weekends. What changed is the number of rooms: three of them now, in three cities, each one built around the same red vinyl booths.

The Midnight Griddle

After the last blue plate goes out, Chef Delfina Arreola clears the pass, sets eight stools and cooks a seven course run straight off the flat top. It is loud, it is close to the heat, and it is the only place we serve the dishes that are still being argued about.

A recent run: dry-aged beef fat toast, green chile clam chowder shot, smashed lamb slider, hominy and marrow, charred cabbage with malt vinegar, a single perfect patty melt, and a hot milk cake with burnt honey.

  • $85 per person, gratuity included in the ticket
  • Optional beverage flight $40, or bring your own bottle for a $20 corkage
  • Dietary needs handled if you tell us 72 hours ahead
